# Narrative audit: StoryScope findings as editorial checks

Source: Russell et al., *StoryScope: Investigating idiosyncrasies in AI fiction*, [arXiv:2604.03136v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03136v6), revised 10 August 2026. Read with the [evidence ledger](evidence-and-sources.md).

## How to use the numbers

Review every applicable check in a substantial fiction draft. The required action is to consider the choice, not to force the opposite of an AI average. Keep a choice that serves the genre, narrator, or explicit brief. A linear story, openly stated feeling, moral, or tidy ending can be excellent human writing.

The table below covers all **30 distinct core features** in Table 16. That table contains **33 outcome rows** because reference explicitness, emotional expression, and subplot integration each have two reported outcomes. Human and AI columns reproduce the displayed values, not target proportions for a new story. AI values pool or average the five studied models as described in the paper.

**Percentages** mean tagged prevalence, **S** means a mean on a 1–5 scale, and **O** means a mean over encoded ordinal categories. Ordinal codes are not percentages or necessarily the same scale as the prose questionnaire. Displayed rounded values sometimes differ from the paper's separately rounded gap column; do not recompute a precise effect from them. Editorial checks are Humanizy's interpretations, not interventions the researchers tested.

## Thirty core checks

| ID | Measured feature and unit | Human / AI | Editorial check |
|---|---|---|---|
| R01 | Thematic explicitness and moralizing, S | 3.28 / 3.94 | Let consequential action carry meaning. Remove the explanatory lesson if the scene already conveys it. Preserve an explicitly requested moral. |
| R02 | Moral/philosophical weighting, S | 3.26 / 3.68 | Give characters immediate wants beyond representing an ethical position. Keep philosophical fiction philosophical. |
| R03 | Thematic unity, S | 4.41 / 4.74 | Check whether every object, incident, and person has been made to symbolize the same idea. Permit details with independent functions. |
| R04 | Narrator explains theme, % | 52 / 77 | Check commentary outside the characters' perspectives. Cut redundant interpretation; retain purposeful narrative judgment. “Never state the moral” is not the finding. |
| R05 | Dialogue used for philosophical debate, % | 34 / 59 | Let speech negotiate, conceal, interrupt, transact, or fail to connect when the situation calls for it. Avoid giving everyone an essay about the theme. |
| R06 | Reference explicitness: implicit echoes; balanced mix, % | 50 / 72; 37 / 16 | Make relevant references definite enough to matter. Vary explicit and implicit reference naturally. Do not insert name-drops to meet a quota. |
| R07 | Dominant emotion: embodied expression; explicit labels, % | 38 / 81; 29 / 8 | Choose among plain emotional naming, behavior, sensation, and implication. Remove repeated tightening chests or similar default bodily metaphors. |
| R08 | Setting as psychological mirror, S | 3.58 / 4.07 | Allow the world to have conditions independent of the protagonist's mood. Use pathetic fallacy deliberately, not in every scene. |
| R09 | Environmental/ecological emphasis, S | 2.83 / 3.21 | Keep environmental attention proportional to this story. Ecology can be central when the subject requires it. |
| R10 | Olfactory imagery present, % | 57 / 82 | Keep smells that locate, change, or reveal something. Remove automatic scent inventories. This is prevalence, not number of smell descriptions. |
| R11 | Sensory density, S | 3.66 / 3.93 | Choose revealing sensory detail. Do not illustrate every perception or reduce all description merely because a mean is higher. |
| R12 | Depth of interior access, S | 3.67 / 3.93 | Decide what the viewpoint can know. Leave some motives or reactions inferable; preserve interiority where it is the story's substance. |
| R13 | Causal-chain continuity, S | 3.92 / 4.20 | Check whether every beat follows too conveniently. Allow credible interruptions, competing causes, and incomplete knowledge without introducing plot holes. |

| R15 | Resolution driven by protagonist choice, % | 46 / 69 | Let other people, institutions, chance, and constraints retain causal force. Do not make every ending solely the reward for the protagonist's insight. |
| R16 | Character introduced through external description, % | 30 / 52 | Consider introduction through speech, action, a relationship, or a consequence. Keep a descriptive entrance when it does useful work. |
| R17 | Subplots: absent; thematically parallel, % | 57 / 79; 42 / 21 | For a story with room, consider a secondary desire or conflict with its own momentum. Integrate it meaningfully. Do not bolt a subplot onto a short post or miniature. |
| R18 | Resolution through internal understanding, % | 27 / 47 | Check whether acceptance alone resolves an external problem. Let consequences remain when insight cannot undo them. |
| R19 | Opening spatial grounding, O | 2.12 / 2.33 | Start where pressure or curiosity begins. Give orientation when needed instead of automatically opening with a setting tour. |
| R20 | Character investment before threat, S | 2.76 / 2.99 | Avoid obligatory biography before the disturbance. Build investment through choices and events as well as prior exposition. |

| R25 | Chronological discontinuity, S | 2.40 / 2.12 | Consider whether a purposeful time shift improves tension or understanding. Keep the timeline reconstructable. |

## Source-specific defaults to examine

These findings concern **Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.4, Gemini 3 Flash, DeepSeek V3.2, and Kimi K2.5** in this corpus. They are not timeless claims about a provider or today's model. Use them to inspect a draft, not to guess its author.

- **Claude:** Section 5 reports the flattest escalation, most uniform narrative voice, more epilogues, and avoidance of dreams. Its reverent/continuist treatment of tradition is reported at 62%, versus 39–56% elsewhere. Check whether events genuinely alter pressure, whether speakers differ for a reason, and whether a quiet ending or epilogue is earned. Do not force an action climax or a dream.
- **GPT:** Gossip/rumor as a plot device appears in 64%, versus 44–55% for other sources. Distant retrospective narration, ensemble-oriented social networks, ambiguous reconciliations, and expectation subversion (41%, versus 27–36% in other AI) also appear. Check whether social information has become the automatic engine; choose the frame and reconciliation for this story. The results-section tell is gossip, not an assertion that GPT uniquely favors dreams.
- **Gemini:** Section 5 reports especially tidy endings, extended denouements, and bleak/oppressive settings in 88%. Check closure length, unresolved consequences, mood variety, and default descriptive entrances. A bleak setting remains valid for a story that needs it.
- **DeepSeek:** Section 5 describes crucial context arriving early. Examine where information becomes necessary and where later disclosure would change interpretation. Table 17 also identifies evenly interleaved backstory as a fingerprint category; this is a different feature, not a reason to claim all context occurs at the beginning.
- **Kimi:** Few identified fingerprints and comparatively weak attribution performance. The compact table names in-action introductions, in-medias-res openings, and absence of explicit trait labeling. These are not defects to reverse. Check specificity of the whole design rather than constructing an “anti-Kimi” recipe.
- **Human:** The human class also has fingerprints. Table 17 includes in-dialogue introductions, single focalization, no direct narrator address, back-loaded revelation, and crossover-genre ambition. These do not all align with pooled human/AI directions in Table 16. A multiclass fingerprint and a binary group tendency answer different questions.

## Structural pass

Write down, privately, what changes, who wants what, what the reader knows when, and what the ending actually resolves. Review the checks above in coherent groups: meaning (R01–06, R21–23), embodiment/setting (R07–14, R19, R28), agency/plot (R15–18, R20, R30), and information/interaction (R24–27, R29). Grouping is an editorial convenience.

For each relevant concern, identify a concrete scene or passage and one repair. Keep counterexamples where the choice is intentional. Ensure the revised sequence still makes sense, names and relationships are consistent, and the ending fulfills the chosen genre's promise. An open thread must be meaningful; an accidentally forgotten plot obligation is a defect.

In a collection or recurring posts, compare premises, speakers, social settings, conflicts, disclosure patterns, and endings across pieces. Look for repeated defaults at the level of the whole collection. Do not use rare combinations or randomness as substitutes for a worthwhile idea.
